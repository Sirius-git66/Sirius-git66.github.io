Why conventional ROI fails AI

NPV was never designed for this asset class.

Conventional NPV and IRR analysis favours the lower-risk option and leaves longer-horizon capability building under-resourced. A synthesis of recent publications from McKinsey, BCG, Harvard Business Review, Deloitte and KPMG — set against real company outcomes — keeps surfacing the same four themes.

01

Single-point metrics have limits

A point estimate invites an argument about whose number is right — a contest the reviewer usually loses to whoever has the better deck.

02

Classify by type and horizon

Cost take-out and capability building are different assets. Discounting both through the same hurdle starves the one that compounds.

03

Driver-based scenarios, not claims

“Thirty per cent productivity” is a claim. Adoption × attribution × realisation × cash-conversion is a case.

04

Force the portfolio discussion

Ranked in isolation, every AI pilot looks marginal. Beside mandatory CapEx, compliance spend and nearer-term projects, the conversation changes.

The method · 01

Every benefit passes through four filters.

A gross benefit claim is reduced by four named factors before any part of it reaches the accounts. Each factor is a separate driver with its own source, evidence tier and accountable signer — so the reduction is documented, not assumed.

01

Adoption

Take-up follows a ramp to a plateau below full coverage. Benefit accrues only on transactions the tool actually touches.

60,000 62% adoption
02

Attribution

The share of the improvement caused by the investment, not by concurrent process change. Isolated by a holdout where one was run; recorded as an estimate where it wasn’t.

42,000 70% attribution
03

Realisation

Output accepted without rework. Human review time is deducted here, once, and never counted again as a cost — the two bases reconcile exactly.

37,800 90% realisation
04

Cash-conversion

The proportion of released capacity that reaches the P&L — an organisational decision, not a modelling output, and the factor most often omitted entirely.

9,450 25% cash-conversion
9,450 hours reaching the P&L · 9.8% of gross

The four factors operate whether or not a business case records them. Recording them assigns each an owner and an evidence tier — the difference between an assumption discovered after the money moves and one audited before it.

The method · 02

Ask what must hold.

Instead of asking “what is the NPV?”, the instrument asks “what must hold for NPV ≥ 0?” and solves each answer in closed form, on every keystroke. Approval becomes a decision about whether the thresholds will hold — which is monitorable, unlike a point estimate.

01

What must hold

For every driver, the value at which the case stops working. “This needs adoption above 55% and you are forecasting 62%” is a statement someone can be held to.

02

Where the case is thin

Each driver is tagged measured, benchmarked or estimated. Narrow headroom on an estimated driver is the real risk, whatever the headline number says.

03

Who signs for it

Every driver carries one accountable name and the document it came from. Not a committee, not a function — a person who can act if the number drifts.

The method · 03

Approval is not the last point of scrutiny.

Research on failed initiatives points one way: the projects that should have stopped at month six typically ran to month twenty-four. The barrier was rarely money — it was that no one had agreed in advance what would count as stopping.

At the gate

Kill criteria are signed before funding. Agreed while nobody yet knows whose project will trigger them. Invoking one later executes an agreement rather than accusing a colleague.

Every month

Each threshold becomes a tripwire. Actuals are compared to the value at which the case fails, on a red, amber and green band. The comparison is arithmetic, not opinion.

On red

A gate review is obliged. It does not stop the initiative. It removes the discretion to say nothing.

On revision

The original is kept beside the revised. A case re-baselined three times is visible as such. Without that, serial revision quietly turns a failing case into a passing one.

In the room

Answers the objections before they’re raised.

“AI benefits are too soft to discount.”

Every benefit passes through four named factors, each with a source and an owner. No blended productivity uplift. Estimates are flagged, not hidden — the confidence meter shows how much of the number you are approving is measured versus estimated.

“It’s another black box.”

No macros, no hidden cells, no protected logic. Every figure traces through named links to a labelled input — and the verifier ships with every engagement, so your team can re-derive the scores itself.

“IT will take six months to clear it.”

There is nothing to clear. No server, no database, no egress, no macros. Serve the folder on the intranet and it runs — a posture that answers enterprise security objections rather than fighting them.

“This is a headcount-reduction tool.”

Headcount avoided is a capacity assumption at full adoption, expressed as a neutral redeployment proportion. The model produces the number; the staffing conversation belongs to the organisation, later.

“The case will be re-baselined into passing.”

Re-baselining keeps the original thresholds beside the revised ones, and gate snapshots are never overwritten. Serial revision is visible as such — which is what stops it.

“Risk belongs in the discount rate.”

Risk lives in scenarios, not in the cost of capital. Base, upside and downside legs are probability-weighted into the approval number; the discount rate stays a cost-of-capital parameter.

Scope & limitations

Honest about where it binds.

where it binds

Operate and Expand-style AI investment with observable operational metrics — adoption counts, transaction volumes, cycle times. The thresholds bite because the metrics already exist.

where it doesn’t

Early-stage, pure-Innovate R&D, where baselines barely exist. There it frames a scenario discussion — it will not manufacture false precision.

It complements standard NPV / IRR discipline. It does not replace it.
